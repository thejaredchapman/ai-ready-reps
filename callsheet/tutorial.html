Lesson 1 · 1 minute

Walk in prepared. Walk out with the follow-up written.

Callsheet is a set of instructions for Claude, plus a small tool that reads public company data. It helps at two moments in your day.

Before a first call

A one-page account brief

  • Three lines to read in the car: why now, what to lead with, what to ask first
  • Who you're meeting: their role and what they've said publicly for work
  • News from the last 90 days, and an industry watch: what happened at their peers and how it may affect them
  • What they're hiring for, five discovery questions, the objections to expect
After the call

Your follow-up, done

  • A follow-up email under 120 words, a CRM note and one task
  • Send it now or at a time you pick. It's held if they reply first
  • Book the next meeting on your calendar
  • Notes and tasks go to HubSpot or Salesforce
  • Nothing saved, booked or sent until you say "approve"
Between calls

Never lose the thread

  • "Where are we with Northwind?": emails, calls, promises and what's overdue
  • Reply drafts and short follow-up series
  • An activity log and time sheet in Google Sheets
  • Ask by voice: "Hey Siri, Callsheet", including "navigate to my 2pm"
  • Turn a call recording into notes, on your computer
  • Expenses in your own words, sent to Concur or Expensify when you approve
What you no longer do
  • Open ten tabs of filings, job boards and news before every call
  • Wonder where a fact came from. Every line has a link, or it's in a Verify before the call list
  • Rewrite the same recap three times for the email, the CRM and your task list

Lesson 5 · 1 minute

What it won't do, and what you'll see instead

These rules come from the AI Ready Reps guardrails. Red means never. Amber means allowed with a check.

Your part

Read before you approve

Approving a send means you've read every word, the recipients and the time. A fingerprint locks exactly that.

Hypotheses stay hypotheses

"Likely priority" means two job posts hint at it. Ask about it; don't state it.

Read the verify list

Every time. A number you can't trace is a number you don't say.

Rate the brief

Reply "rate 4, the trigger event was last year's". Ratings make the next one better.

Lesson 6 · 1 minute

Your first two weeks

Move up one rung only when the rung below has run cleanly.

Week 1
Read and draft. Run briefs and follow-ups. Log your calls. Approve nothing. Send the drafts yourself from Gmail.
Week 2
Write with approval. Approve notes and tasks into HubSpot or Salesforce, and schedule single follow-ups. Spot-check afterward.
Week 3
Series with approval. Use follow-up series once your scheduled sends have gone out cleanly for a week.
Never
Write alone. Callsheet isn't built for unattended writes. Customer-facing work stays below this rung.

When something looks wrong

The wrong company
Give the ticker: "prep Northwind, ticker NWND"
"No public job board found"
Normal for some companies. The priorities section will be thin. That's honest, not broken.
A flagged instruction in the verify list
Nothing to do. Callsheet ignored it. Tell whoever runs the tool.
A fact you know is wrong
Don't use it. Reply with the correction so it can be checked against the source.
Lesson 7 · 2 minutes

Between calls: the thread, the log, your voice

The work between calls is where deals stall. Three things keep them moving.

Correspondence

"Where are we with Northwind?"

Callsheet reads the email thread, the HubSpot deal or Salesforce opportunity, your log and your outbox, then tells you: what you promised, what they asked, what's overdue, and one next step. It can draft the reply, or set up a short follow-up series that stops the moment they answer.

Voice

"Hey Siri, Callsheet"

Then say: "prep me for my 2pm", "navigate to my 2pm", "log a 20-minute call with the Northwind IT lead about security review", "what's scheduled this week?", or "cancel the Northwind email". Answers are spoken and saved, and directions open in Maps. Approvals still happen on screen.

Recordings

From recording to follow-up

If everyone agreed to be recorded, Callsheet transcribes the call on your computer (the audio never leaves it). Then a reviewer agent turns it into notes: their pains in their own words, what they asked, what you promised, the next step. Paste the notes into a follow-up.

Make it better

"Make this better"

A reviewer agent opens the sources behind a brief or follow-up, moves anything unsupported to the verify list, merges repeats and tightens the wording. You get the improved version and a list of what changed.

Your activity log is also your time sheet

Every call you log and every email Callsheet sends adds a row, in a local file and your Google Sheet. Ask "my time this week" for a total. Say "log my week in Salesforce" to copy it there too, after you approve the preview.

Lesson 8 · 2 minutes

On the road: less driving, cheaper gas, expenses done

Say "plan my day". Callsheet reads your calendar and puts the in-person meetings in the order that drives the fewest miles. Meetings with a set time stay put.

#StopWhenDriveLeave by

Group by area

"Save gas this week"

Callsheet spots meetings that sit near each other on different days and suggests pairing them. Moving a meeting means asking the prospect, so it drafts the reschedule email for you to approve.

Gas prices

"Cheapest gas near my 2pm"

Live prices from nearby stations, cheapest first, each with when it was last updated, plus a Maps link. Route costs use the local average.

Receipts by camera

Snap, confirm, done

Tap the Callsheet Receipt Shortcut, take the photo, and it's read for you: merchant, date, total. You confirm, and it's logged with the photo attached. Card numbers are never stored.

Mileage

"Log today's route"

Miles are worked out from the addresses and priced at your company's rate. "Expenses this month" gives totals by category and flags missing receipts.

Sample day, made-up stops; distances and prices are illustrative. Routes and mileage are for business locations only, never a person's home.

Lesson 9 · 2 minutes

Expenses: say it your way, send it when it's ready

Every business expense can be logged, however you say it. If something's missing, it's logged anyway and flagged, so nothing falls through. Try it:

Never lost

Flagged, not refused

A lunch without a business purpose is still logged. Callsheet asks for the purpose and who attended, and won't send it for reimbursement until they're filled in. Possible duplicates are flagged too.

All at once

Import a card statement

Download the CSV from your card. Callsheet previews it, sorts each charge (Uber → rideshare, Shell → fuel), skips card payments and anything already logged, then logs the rest when you say yes.

Sending for reimbursement

Say "send my November expenses to Concur". You see exactly what will go, and what's held back and why. Nothing is sent until you say yes, and nothing is ever sent twice. Expensify and other systems (Ramp, Brex, Navan and more) work the same way.
