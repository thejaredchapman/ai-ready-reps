## 4. The traffic-light rule

| Light | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Green (safe) | Your own writing or public information, in an approved tool, with your review of the result | Tighten an email you wrote |
| Amber (guardrails) | Touches customer or company data. Allowed only with the listed control and sign-off | Summarize a recorded call with consent |
| Red (not safe) | Breaks confidentiality, privacy, consent, or honesty. Do not do it | Paste a customer contract into an unapproved tool |

## 5. Three questions before any prompt

1. **What data goes in?** Public, internal, or customer/regulated. Customer and regulated data needs written approval.
2. **Who checks the output?** A named person reads it and verifies every fact, number, name, and claim before it reaches a customer.
3. **What if it is wrong?** Low impact is a clumsy draft. Medium is a wrong fact in a customer email. High is a pricing, legal, security, or compliance statement and needs expert sign-off.

## 6. Data classification

| Class | Examples | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Websites, press releases, filings | May be used in approved tools |
| Internal | Playbooks, non-public pricing, forecasts | Approved enterprise tools only |
| Customer or confidential | Contracts, personal data, customer lists | Only with written approval from [LEGAL/IT] |
| Regulated | Health, payment, government ID, financial data | Never, unless explicitly approved for that tool and use |

## 7. Rules for everyone

- Use only company-approved accounts. Never use personal accounts for work data.
- Share the minimum needed, and replace names with placeholders when you can.
- Never put passwords, keys, payment details, or health data into a prompt.
- Verify every fact, number, quote, and claim against a trusted source before use.
- Do not invent references, testimonials, or statistics.
- Do not let AI set prices, discounts, or contract terms.
- Follow call-recording and consent rules for every region you sell in.
- Disclose AI use where law or contract requires it, and never deny being an AI when someone sincerely asks.
- Keep a person approving anything an AI agent sends or changes outside your account.
- Report mistakes fast. Early reports are valued.

## 9. Prompt library

Replace the [brackets]. Use only public or approved content, in an approved tool.

### 1. Account research brief (Prospecting)

```text
Act as a sales researcher. Using only the public text I paste below about [company], write a six-bullet brief: what they do, recent news, likely priorities, and three questions I could ask. Flag anything you are unsure about. [paste public text]
```

**Check before use:** Verify every fact against the source you pasted.

### 2. First-touch email (Prospecting)

```text
Write a 90-word cold email from me to a [job title] at a [industry] company. Reason for reaching out: [public trigger]. One clear question. No hype, no jargon. Give three subject lines.
```

**Check before use:** Remove any claim you cannot back up, and confirm the trigger is public.

### 3. Connection notes (Prospecting)

```text
Write three LinkedIn connection notes under 250 characters, friendly and specific to [shared professional interest]. No flattery.
```

**Check before use:** Make sure it is relevant, not creepy.

### 4. Buyer concerns (Prospecting)

```text
List ten questions a [role] at a [industry] company is probably asking about [problem area]. Group them by urgency.
```

**Check before use:** Treat as hypotheses and test them on real calls.

### 5. Discovery question bank (Discovery)

```text
Give me twelve discovery questions for a [role] about [problem]. Mix situation, problem, impact, and decision-process questions. Avoid yes/no questions.
```

**Check before use:** Choose the five that fit your style.

**Check before use:** Share it with the buyer ahead of time only if you are happy with it.

5. **Follow instructions,** which may include vendor deletion requests or customer notification.

- If an organization downgrades from an Enterprise plan to a non-Enterprise plan, consumer defaults apply and users must opt out manually. [1]
- **Watch out:** Open the cited sources. A citation does not guarantee the page says what the answer claims.
- **Watch out:** Do not use consumer plans for confidential work data.

Sources:

1. Perplexity, [Data collection at Perplexity (Help Center)](https://intercom.help/perplexity-ai/en/articles/11564572-data-collection-at-perplexity). Article dated March 3, 2026. Opened and read October 14, 2026.

Questions to ask: Which plan covers our team? Can we set custom retention and force deletion? What are the terms for API and search products?

## 14. Rollout plan

**Days 1 to 30**

- Talk to Legal and IT
- Choose approved tools
- Write acceptable-use rules
- Train a pilot team

**Days 31 to 60**

- Run three green use cases
- Add one amber use case with controls
- Build a shared prompt library
- Collect wins and misses

**Days 61 to 90**

- Measure results
- Review with Legal and IT
- Expand to more teams
- Decide what to automate next

### Rollout checklist

- [ ] Ask Legal what data, customers, and regions are off limits for AI tools.
- [ ] Ask IT which AI tools are approved and which plans are covered by a contract.
- [ ] Confirm in writing each vendor's training, retention, and subprocessor terms.
- [ ] Write a one-page acceptable-use policy using the safe, guardrails, and not-safe tiers.
- [ ] Confirm call-recording and transcription consent rules for every region you sell in.
- [ ] Start with a pilot team and three safe use cases.
- [ ] Name an owner who reviews outputs and tracks errors during the pilot.
- [ ] Test the same three real tasks across two or more models and compare results.
- [ ] Add verification steps for numbers, claims, and quotes before anything reaches a customer.
- [ ] Train reps on what never goes into a tool.
- [ ] Decide how AI use is disclosed to customers where required.
- [ ] Review results after 30 days with Legal and IT before expanding.
